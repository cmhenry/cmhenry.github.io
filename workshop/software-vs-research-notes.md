# Software vs. research — presenter notes

Target: 20 minutes. This contrast concerns different acceptance questions, not a claim that software engineers lack rigor or that researchers do not iterate.

1. **Title:** successful execution and defensible inference are distinct.
2. **Premise:** applications have product objectives; identify which are relevant to the research task.
3. **Product practices:** iteration, abstraction, and release criteria can be useful in research too. Do not present an unsupported history of the whole software industry.
4. **Product criteria:** these are examples, not universal maxims. Reliable operation matters.
5. **Research criteria:** add a justified claim, appropriate evidence, and inspectable procedures. These do not replace good engineering.
6. **Comparison:** ask what each criterion tests. A script that runs, a real citation, and a valid inference are separate achievements.
7. **Defaults:** assess provenance, confidence, and completion in the actual output. Do not attribute all confident prose to RLHF or assume all interfaces hide their operations.
8. **Execution and validation:** use a concrete example: a correct program calculating the wrong survey score, or an accurate citation attached to an overbroad claim. Traceable errors remain errors.
9. **Four checks:** traceability links claims to evidence; auditability allows inspection; reproducibility preserves inputs/procedures and examines variation; transparency discloses relevant methods and limitations. None alone establishes validity.
10. **Apply:** use these checks during the practicals. An ordinary response can be useful, a configured workflow can fail, and rejecting a method can be justified.

If time is short, combine the two criteria slides and protect time for the concrete examples. Avoid slogans about speed being inherently unscientific or transparency requiring full access to a model's internal computation.
