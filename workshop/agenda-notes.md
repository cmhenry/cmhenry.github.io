# Agenda — presenter notes

Target: 5–8 minutes. Introduce the research tasks without promising adoption, failure, or a particular model outcome.

## Slides 1–4: purpose and goals

Ask briefly what participants have used AI for and which tasks they want to assess. The workshop compares outputs against research requirements: whether a citation exists and supports a claim, whether a critique follows from a draft, and whether a survey task preserves its intended construct.

Tools can help, fail, or be unsuitable for a particular purpose. Domain judgment remains necessary, but do not claim models categorically cannot contribute to theoretical discussion. The exercise is to inspect their contribution and its limits.

Software engineering and research both use testing and iteration. The difference discussed here is between demonstrating that a system executes and establishing that an inference is justified.

## Slides 5–7: adoption figures

These retained figures come from [Lyttelton, Massenkoff, and Wilmers, Coding agents in the social sciences](https://www.anthropic.com/research/coding-agents-social-sciences). Describe the sample and measures from the original study when presenting them; do not generalize the percentages to all researchers.

The displayed differences describe reported adoption in the study. Do not infer why a group adopts, treat name-derived gender as self-reported gender, or turn adoption/productivity associations into demonstrated causal effects. Higher adoption is not the workshop's success criterion.

This revision retains the existing images; it does not report a new survey analysis. If time is short, discuss one figure and keep the others available for questions.

## Slide 8: sequence

- Framing: models, training, context, and retrieval.
- Research standards: successful execution versus defensible inference.
- Tools: inspect available access and supervision.
- Literature review: retrieve and verify candidate records.
- Synthetic peer review: compare grounded criticism with unsupported claims.
- Survey research: wording, coding open responses, and synthetic cases.
