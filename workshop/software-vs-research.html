The premise

LLM tools are software products

  • Built by software teams, shipped on software timelines.
  • They inherit a set of assumptions about what "good" means.
  • Those assumptions are not neutral. They were chosen to solve a different problem than ours.
  • If we don't surface them, we adopt them.
Where it came from

25 years of converging practice

  • The organizational problem: coordinate teams to ship products under uncertainty.
  • Agile / Scrum — short cycles, rapid iteration, ship early.
  • Abstraction as virtue — a good tool hides complexity. It "just works".
  • Minimum viable product — build the simplest thing, learn from feedback.
  • "Done" is pragmatic: works reliably enough to release.
Software says…

Four maxims, learned by heart

  • Move fast, iterate later.
  • Abstraction is a feature.
  • "Done" = ships and works.
  • Optimize for the user's convenience.

None of these are wrong. They are answers to a specific question.

Research says…

A different four maxims

  • Get it right, then publish.
  • Transparency is an obligation.
  • "Done" = withstands scrutiny.
  • Optimize for epistemic accountability.

Different question, different answers.

Where it bites

The defaults are not yours

  • Confident prose is a UX choice. RLHF rewards it. Research punishes it.
  • Abstraction hides provenance. "It just works" means you can't show how.
  • "Done" in chat means an answer arrived — not that it survives peer review.
  • Convenience moves friction off the user and onto the record.
The trap

It feels productive. That's the problem.

  • The tool is optimized to feel finished.
  • Your discipline is optimized to be finished.
  • Adopting the tool's defaults imports its standards, quietly.
  • The mismatch shows up at review, not at the prompt.
What we need instead

Four criteria for research-grade use

  • Traceability — every claim back to a source.
  • Auditability — someone else can inspect the chain.
  • Reproducibility — re-run and get a defensible result.
  • Transparency — the prompt, the tool, the model, the version, on the record.
Looking ahead

These are the criteria we'll apply

  • Every tool in the next two hours will be judged on these four.
  • Some pass. Some don't. Most pass only with effort.
  • That effort is the work. It is not overhead.
  • The skill we're building today is naming when convenience costs you accountability.
